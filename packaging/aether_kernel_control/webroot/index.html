💡 User Guide Practical Scenarios

❓ AETHER Help Center & Knowledge Base

Real-world scenarios showing exactly how each tweak improves your daily experience on Redmi Note 10:

⚡ What is Bypass Charging?
How it works: Tells the Qualcomm SMB5 charging controller to halt electric current into the battery cells while keeping USB power alive to run the motherboard directly.
💡 Real-World Scenario: BGMI/PUBG Heavy Gaming While Charging
Normally, gaming while plugged into a 33W charger creates intense heat (43°C+) because battery charging generates heat on top of the GPU heat, triggering severe thermal throttling and dropping FPS from 60 to 35. With Bypass Charging enabled, battery charging is bypassed entirely—the phone stays cool (~33°C), battery cycle wear is eliminated, and games stay locked at 60 FPS.
📱 What is AMOLED DC Dimming?
How it works: Replaces Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) with continuous electric current regulation to dim the Samsung Super AMOLED panel.
💡 Real-World Scenario: Reading in Bed in a Dark Room
At brightness levels below 30%, standard AMOLED screens rapidly turn pixels ON and OFF 240 times a second to simulate lower brightness. While invisible consciously, this high-frequency strobe effect causes eye strain, dryness, and tension headaches after 15 minutes of reading. DC Dimming eliminates this pulse flicker completely for soothing night reading.
🎙️ How does In-Call Mic Calibration work?
How it works: Directly calibrates the audio codec unmute timing register in the Qualcomm Bolero WCD937x TX Macro driver.
💡 Real-World Scenario: Answering Incoming Calls
On stock kernels, there is a known hardware timing bug where the microphone stays muted for 50-100ms when picking up a call, leading to callers asking "Hello? Are you there?". AETHER v1.6 permanently calibrates this to 5ms with pre-charge ramp-up, so your very first word is heard loud and clear on pickup.
🎮 Why lock the GPU to 504MHz in Gaming Mode?
How it works: Sets a strict clock frequency floor on the Adreno 612 GPU and extends downclock latency from 80ms to 200ms.
💡 Real-World Scenario: Intense Multiplayer Teamfights
In games like BGMI, COD Mobile, or Genshin Impact, whenever a player looks at the ground or transitions scenes, the default governor drops the GPU clock down to 180MHz to save micro-watts. When an enemy suddenly appears and throws a smoke grenade, the GPU cannot scale up fast enough, causing a stutter drop to 40 FPS. Locking the floor to 504 MHz keeps rendering units warm and ready for guaranteed 60 FPS.
🖐️ What is Touchscreen Glove Mode?
How it works: Modulates the capacitive threshold registers on the FocalTech FT3418 touch digitizer.
💡 Real-World Scenario: Thick Screen Protectors & Winter Wear
If you have installed a thick 9H tempered glass screen protector or use motorcycle riding gloves, touch taps can occasionally fail to register. Turning on Glove Mode triples capacitive sensitivity, allowing smooth taps and gestures even with winter gloves on.
📁 What is UFS Read-Ahead & TCP Fast Open?
How it works: Expands the Linux block I/O lookahead buffer to 512KB and sends payload data during initial TCP handshakes.
💡 Real-World Scenario: App Launch Speeds & Low Game Ping
Setting UFS Read-Ahead to 512 KB pre-loads sequential flash blocks into memory ahead of time, launching Instagram, YouTube, and heavy games up to 30% faster. Combining this with Google BBR and TCP Fast Open eliminates packet queue bufferbloat, keeping gaming ping stable even over congested Wi-Fi.
🛡️ Is SUSFS v2.3.0 fully spoofed?
How it works: Low-level kernel VFS hooks intercept system calls before userspace detection libraries can see them.
💡 Real-World Scenario: Banking Apps, Work Profiles & Play Integrity
Security engines like Google Play Integrity, Momo, RootBeer, and banking apps inspect `/proc/mounts`, bootloader cmdlines, and open syscalls for Magisk/KernelSU footprints. SUSFS v2.3.0 intercepts these calls directly in kernel space, redirecting and unmounting paths invisibly so all banking apps and enterprise work profiles run without detection.
